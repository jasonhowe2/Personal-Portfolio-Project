MLB Hitting vs. Pitching

Is good pitching or good hitting more likely to lead an MLB team to a winning season?

View GitHub RepositoryView Notebook

1. Research Question

Is good pitching or good hitting more likely to lead a team to wins in the MLB?

The machine-learning version predicts whether an MLB team finishes with a winning record. Winning Season = 1 when W > L and 0 otherwise. This makes the project a binary classification problem.

The analysis compares hitting-only, pitching-only, and combined feature sets using Logistic Regression and a Decision Tree.

3. Features

HittingRuns/game, HR/game, OBP, and SLG.
PitchingRuns allowed/game, ERA, and SO/9.
CombinedAll seven selected features.
No leakageWins, losses, and winning percentage are not predictors.

MLB defines OBP, SLG, and ERA as established measures of reaching base, total bases per at-bat, and earned runs allowed per nine innings.

6. What the Results Suggest

When considered separately, pitching/run prevention produced stronger predictive performance than the selected hitting statistics. The best pitching-only model reached 84.4% accuracy compared with 73.3% for the best hitting-only models.

Combining both groups improved performance further. The combined Logistic Regression model reached 87.8% accuracy and an 88.4% F1 score, correctly classifying 79 of 90 held-out team-seasons.

This describes predictive performance, not proof that pitching causes winning.

8. Limitations & Ethics

  • Observational team-season data cannot establish causation.
  • Same-season predictors and outcomes mean this is not a pre-season forecasting system.
  • Defense, baserunning, injuries, roster construction, payroll, park effects, schedule strength, and bullpen usage are omitted.
  • The test period contains only three seasons.
  • The 2020 MLB season was shortened to 60 games.
  • Rules, technology, strategy, and offensive environments changed across 2000–2025.
  • A real-world model used for high-stakes personnel, financial, or betting decisions could have greater consequences when predictions are wrong.
